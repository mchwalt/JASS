Four percussion tracks on a 32-step grid, played dry into the master bus. It runs as soon as the module is switched on — no key needed.

- **The grid** — **left click sets a step and sounds it**, right click clears one; dragging keeps going, so a row of hats is one gesture. Each row is named after the instrument it plays. Every fourth step is shaded; steps past LEN are dimmed and stay silent.
- **KIT** — the sample set the tracks fire from, its own and independent of the SAMPLER's.
- **AMP** (next to KIT) — how loud the whole kit sits against the rest. Centre is the kit as recorded, right of centre boosts it (a drum kit is mastered with headroom, oscillators are not).
- **NOTE 1…4** — which instrument of the track. The read-out names it (Kick, Snare, HH Closed).
- **AMP 1…4** — balance between the tracks. **PAN 1…4** places each one.
- **SYNC / RATE / LEN** — step length as a note division (or free), and how many steps before the pattern repeats. LEN 16 on 1/8 is two bars.

The drums are the clock: with PERC running, a STEP SEQ figure started from silence enters on the next start of the drum pattern instead of wherever you pressed the key.

PERC never passes through the filter or the effects — those live in the voices, and the beat is not one. A preset whose kit is still loading stays silent rather than play the wrong one.
