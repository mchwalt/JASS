A second voice beside the patch: one sequenced bass, played by the STEP SEQ while the keyboard plays the patch. It has its own oscillator, filter and envelope and goes straight to the master bus — the patch's filter, delay and reverb do not touch it.

To use it: switch BASS on, then switch **BASS** on in the STEP SEQ. The figure now plays here, transposed by the key you play, and the key's chord plays the patch.

- **WAVE** — sawtooth or square.
- **SUB** — a square one octave down.
- **CUTOFF** — the filter's rest position; **RESO** — its peak.
- **ENV** — how fast the filter falls back after each note.
- **ENV>CUT** — how far a note opens the filter. An accented step opens it further (STEP SEQ's ACCENT).
- **GLIDE** — how long a SLIDE step takes to reach the next note. A TIE retunes at once.
- **LEVEL**, **PAN** — its place in the mix.

One box, one job: no matrix targets here, no second BASS. More voices of music are more JASS instances on more tracks in your DAW.
